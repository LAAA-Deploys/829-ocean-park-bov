Market Context

Nine closed sales of five to twelve unit buildings in 90405 since June 2025 frame the subject. The five to seven unit sales closed at $2,170,000 to $2,525,000, and where income was reported, at cap rates of 5.00% to 6.07% and multipliers of 11.58 to 12.89. The two most instructive are 824 Maple St, directly behind the subject, at $2,525,000 and a 5.87% cap in May 2026, and 2332 20th St, a 1959 six-unit of similar mix, at $2,210,000 and a 6.07% cap in July 2026.

The active listings are the other half of the picture. 2648 23rd St, an eight-unit 1949 building with the same one and two bedroom mix and seven renovated units, asks $3.2M at a published 5.50% cap. 1937 17th St, a six-unit 1948 building with two vacancies, asks $2.25M at 5.00%. The subject at $2.45M sits between them on price, below both on price per square foot, and offers a fully occupied roll that neither does.

Positioning Thesis

The right buyer values what is scarce here: a corner lot a mile from the beach, seven paying tenants, a clean title history and two units whose rents reset on turnover. That buyer should be qualified early on whether they will hold for the turnover or want it priced in, because the answer decides whether an offer lands at the top or the bottom of the range.

Likely Buyer Profiles

Likely Buyer 01

Westside Small-Building Owner

Owners who already hold five to fifteen unit buildings in Santa Monica, Venice and Mar Vista, including the buyers on the Maple, 20th Street and Grant Street sales this year. They know the registry, the General Adjustment and the tenant base, and they buy on yield against a known turnover path.

Likely Buyer 02

1031 Exchange Buyer

Sellers trading out of larger or more management-intensive assets into a Westside replacement property under a 45-day identification clock. The 2332 20th St buyer in July was completing an exchange, and a fully occupied Santa Monica corner building at a 5.24% yield is exactly the identification that pool makes.

Likely Buyer 03

Long-Hold Family Investor

Families and individuals who buy Santa Monica apartments to hold for decades, the same profile as the current ownership. They pay for location and tenant stability rather than the first-year yield, and they are the buyers most likely to reach the top of the range.

Transaction Strategy

01

Price Off the Sale Behind the Building

824 Maple St shares the block with the subject and closed in May 2026 at $2,525,000, a 5.87% cap and an 11.58 multiplier. Every buyer who tours will know that sale. Pricing at $2.45M, a 5.24% cap and an 11.93 multiplier on a smaller building with more rent upside sits deliberately in the shadow of that print rather than above it, and the offering should say so rather than let a buyer discover it.

02

Sell the Turnover Story Honestly

Two of the seven units rent at $1,712 and $1,757 against a one-bedroom market of about $2,500. That is roughly $1,500 per month of income that arrives only when those tenants leave, and no one can promise when. The offering should show the current column and the market column side by side, state that the gap is turnover-dependent under Santa Monica rent control, and let the buyer price the timing. Buyers who find that gap on their own treat it as a problem; buyers who are shown it treat it as the reason to buy.

03

Have the Registry and the Bills Ready Before Launch

The documents a Santa Monica buyer asks for on the first call are the Rent Control Board registration for each unit, twelve months of bills, and the seismic retrofit status. Ownership has provided a rent roll and one month of expenses. Assembling the registry printout, a trailing statement and the City's retrofit letter before launch removes the three questions that stall escrows on buildings of this vintage.
